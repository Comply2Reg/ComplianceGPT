[order=17] [label=text] The  European  Securities  and  Markets  Authority  (ESMA),  the  EU's  financial markets regulator and supervisor, has published its takeaways from the 2025 Call for Evidence (CfE) on the retail investor journey.
[order=18] [label=text] Taking  into  account  the  input  from  stakeholders,  ESMA  outlines  a  number  of actions and operational improvements it will take forward to make it easier for retail investors to access suitable investment opportunities.
[order=19] [label=text] ESMA will focus on three areas:
[order=20] [label=text] streamlining  disclosure  requirements and tackling  information  overload  for investors;
[order=21] [label=text] reducing complexity in suitability and appropriateness assessments;
[order=22] [label=text] simplifying MiFID II requirements on sustainability preferences.
[order=23] [label=text] As  part  of  this  follow  up  work,  consumer  testing  will  be  used  to  inform  and validate  improvements  to  disclosures  and  digital  investor  journeys,  including for mobile-first users.

[order=87] [label=text] The  cost  and  performance  of  products  are  key  determinants  of  the  benefits retail investors in the EU can get from their investments. Clear and comprehensive information enables investors to assess costs and past performance and supports informed decision-making and retail participation in capital markets. [order=88] [label=text] Read Market report on Costs and performance of EU retail investment products 2025 - Annex
[order=89] [label=section_header] The key findings in the report are:
[order=90] [label=list_item] UCITS costs declined gradually, driven mainly by new funds, with ongoing costs falling by 8% for retail equity funds, and by almost 15% for retail bond funds. Cost reductions were more limited for existing funds, at 3% for equity funds and 9% for bond funds.
[order=91] [label=list_item] UCITS performance improved significantly in 2024. Equity and mixed funds achieved  their  second-best  results  since  2020,  while  bond  funds  reached their highest level of returns. [order=92] [label=list_item] ESG UCITS continued to have lower costs than non-ESG. However, in 2024, ESG  funds  underperformed  their  non-ESG  equivalents.  Similarly,  funds classified under SFDR Article 9 recorded lower returns than Article 6 funds.
[order=93] [label=list_item] Alternative  Investment  Funds  (AIFs)  remained  dominated  by  professional investors, and between 2022 and 2024 the share of retail investors investing in  these  products  decreased  from  14%  to  9%.  Annual  net  returns  were positive across all categories of AIFs in 2024.
[order=94] [label=list_item] Structured  Retail  Product  costs  remained  broadly  stable  in  2024,  while interest-rate linked products continued to gain market share, reaching 27% - up from just 1% in 2021. [order=142] [label=section_header] ESMA consults on post-trade risk reduction services under EMIR 3
[order=143] [label=text] The  European  Securities  and  Markets  Authority  (ESMA),  the  EU's  financial markets  regulator  and  supervisor,  has  launched  a  consultation  on  the requirements for how post-trade risk reduction (PTRR) services can benefit from  the  conditioned  exemption  from  the  clearing  obligation  introduced under the European Market Infrastructure Regulation (EMIR 3).
[order=144] [label=text] ESMA is seeking feedback on several elements of the framework for the PTRR service  providers  to  operate  under  the  exemption,  including  transparency towards  participants,  algorithm  safeguards,  execution  of  PTRR  exercises, controls to be  performed  and  record  keeping.  Finally, the consultation describes how monitoring should be conducted by the relevant authorities.
[order=145] [label=text] The draft Regulatory Technical Standards (RTS) set out the requirements that PTRR services must meet for over-the-counter (OTC) derivative transactions to qualify for the exemption from the clearing obligation. They focus on the three main  service types in use in the market  today: compression,  portfolio rebalancing and basis risk optimisation.
[order=146] [label=text] The RTS are designed to ensure that the exemption is not used to circumvent the clearing obligation, while considering simplification and burden reduction objectives  by  leveraging  on  current  practices  since  the  start  of  the  EMIR  3 regime.
[order=147] [label=section_header] Next steps
[order=148] [label=text] Stakeholders are invited to provide feedback on the proposals set out in the consultation by 20 April 2026. The draft RTS will be submitted to the European Commission in Q4 2026.

[order=152] [label=section_header] ESMA sets out clearing thresholds under EMIR 3
[order=153] [label=text] The  European  Securities  and  Markets  Authority  (ESMA),  the  EU's  financial markets regulator and supervisor, has published its draft Regulatory Technical  Standards  (RTS)  setting  out  new  and  revised  clearing  thresholds (CTs)  under  EMIR  3.  The  proposed  thresholds  ensure  continuity  in  the coverage of systemic risk in over - the - counter (OTC) derivative markets while avoiding  unnecessary  complexity  and  additional  compliance  burdens  for market participants.
[order=154] [label=section_header] To reduce unnecessary complexity and burden, ESMA has:
[order=155] [label=list_item] retained  five  CTs  categories,  avoiding  additional categories or more granular thresholds;
[order=156] [label=list_item] clarified the timing of calculation of positions, allowing counterparties to apply the new CTs during their usual assessment window  or  earlier,  if  they  wish  to  benefit  sooner  from  the  new regime;
[order=157] [label=list_item] enhanced  stability  and  visibility  in  the  mechanism triggering the review of the CT.
[order=158] [label=text] Additionally,  ESMA  suggests  increasing  the  thresholds  in  the  commodity, interest  rate  and  credit  derivatives  asset  classes  compared  to  what  was proposed in the Consultation Paper published in April 2025. These adjustments reflect  recent  price  developments,  inflation  and  other  relevant  market  factors while ensuring a proportionate coverage of the systemic risk.
[order=159] [label=text] Although  respondents  to  the  consultation  requested  broader  recognition  of structured hedging arrangements, including virtual power purchase agreement (VPPAs),  ESMA  confirms  that  any  change  to  the  hedging  exemption  would require amendments at Regulation level and therefore cannot be addressed in these RTS.

[order=168] [label=section_header] The EBA and ESMA consult on revised suitability assessment requirements for banks and investment firms
[order=169] [label=text] The  European  Banking  Authority  (EBA)  and  the  European  Securities  and Markets Authority (ESMA) today launched a consultation on the revised joint guidelines on the assessment of the suitability of members of the management body and key function holders. The revised guidelines form part of  a  broader  package  designed  to  harmonise  suitability  assessments  and ensure supervisory convergence across the EU. The consultation runs until 25 May 2026.
[order=170] [label=text] The draft  revised  EBA-ESMA  joint  guidelines  new  requirements  introduced  by the  revised  Capital  Requirements  Directive  (CRD)  for  large  institutions,  and include  updates  applying  to  entities  covered  by  CRD  and  to  investment  firms within the scope  of Markets  in Financial Instruments  Directive (MiFID II). Stemming  from  CRD,  the  updates  cover,  among  other  elements,  the  use  of ex - ante  applications  for  cases  where  competent  authorities  carry  out  ex - post assessments, and mandatory suitability assessments for roles such as heads of control functions and chief financial officers. The guidelines also further specify new CRD requirements for third - country branches, and strengthen links with the anti-money  laundering  and  countering  the  financing  of  terrorism  framework, providing  guidance  for  identifying  reasonable  grounds  to  suspect  money laundering or terrorist financing risks.
[order=171] [label=text] The  revised  guidelines  introduce  targeted  simplification  and  streamlining measures  designed  to  reduce  administrative  burden  and  provide  greater flexibility and clarity for institutions and supervisors.
